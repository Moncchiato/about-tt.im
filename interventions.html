Affect Tolerance

Affect varies along two axes: energy (arousal) and valence. When some regions of that space feel intolerable — high-energy distress like panic or rage, low-energy states like emptiness or boredom, even calm or joy — clients escape them through avoidance, numbing, impulsive action or dissociation. Widening the window of tolerance lets a client stay present and regulated across the full affective range.

Guiding question: Does the client have the capacity to handle a range of affective experiences, including all combinations of high and low energy and high and low valence?
15 options
Somatic Experiencing Somatic
Titrate activation in small doses and pendulate between distress and safety to gradually stretch nervous-system capacity.
Sensorimotor Psychotherapy Somatic
Track posture, movement and autonomic shifts at the edges of the window; build physical containment and grounding.
Integral Somatic Psychology Somatic
Spread emotional resonance across more of the body so an emotion feels less concentrated and easier to hold.
Trauma-informed yoga Somatic
Build interoceptive awareness and tolerance for fear-linked sensations without dissociating.
DBT Distress Tolerance DBT
TIPP, self-soothing and radical acceptance to ride out acute high-energy spikes without impulsive action.
DBT Emotion Regulation DBT
Reduce emotional vulnerability so intense affect arrives less often and is easier to stay with.
ACT willingness & defusion ACT
Target experiential avoidance; make room for uncomfortable affect while acting on values.
Emotion Regulation Therapy CBT
Mindful attention regulation plus metacognitive reframing to allow anxiety, anger or sadness instead of worrying or ruminating.
STAIR Trauma-Focused
Builds emotional awareness and modulation skills before trauma narrative work; designed for complex trauma.
AEDP / ISTDP Psychodynamic
Treat 'affect phobia': name the defenses against core emotions, then experience them to completion in a safe dyad.
Emotion-Focused Therapy Experiential
Deepen emotional processing in session, rather than intellectualizing or bypassing, to transform maladaptive emotion schemes.
Parts Work IFS
Unblend from overwhelmed or protective 'parts' so intense states can be witnessed with curiosity rather than flooding.
EMDR Trauma-Focused
Process distressing memories with dual attention while staying anchored in the present.
Positive Affect Tolerance (PAT) protocol Trauma-Focused
EMDR adaptation for clients who can't tolerate praise, warmth or connection — common after neglect.
Accelerated Resolution Therapy Trauma-Focused
Eye movements with imagery rescripting to reduce physiological reactivity and expand emotional resilience.

Metabolic Health

Circadian Rhythm

Cortisol rhythm and sleep-wake timing can be disrupted even when total sleep is intact — a distinct target. Modern light, screens, and shift schedules are common culprits.

Guiding question: Ample early-morning sunlight?
Guiding question: Energized during daylight hours?

Cross-cutting

Executive Functioning

Not a pillar — you don't need it to feel positive emotion — but it influences nearly every outcome and every session. Scaffold it rather than assume it can be permanently 'trained.'

Guiding question: Any executive-functioning deficits? (e.g., screen with the BRIEF2.)
6 options
CBT for ADHD CBT
Skills for planning, initiation, and follow-through.
Environmental / external systems Behavioral
Offload EF onto structure, reminders, and checklists.
Implementation intentions Behavioral
'If-then' plans bridge intention and action.
Coaching & scaffolding Behavioral
External support during skill-building.
Bottom-up regulation Somatic
Regulate arousal first when cognition is intact but affect isn't.
Stimulant / non-stimulant medication Psychiatric
Improve EF capacity where clinically indicated.
