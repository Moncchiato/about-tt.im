Targets by Diagnosis

Sample combinations of TTIM treatment targets for common psychiatric disorders — each with the reason it so often belongs in the formulation.

i

Here are some sample Treatment Target combinations for common psychiatric disorders. These are meant to be samples of possible combinations, and not govern the decision making process for every individual with a specific diagnosis. Various combinations of Treatment Targets will be necessary for different individuals at different points in treatment. You can start with a diagnosis, or you can start with Treatment Targets and reverse engineer them back to a diagnosis.

Neurodevelopmental

Attention-Deficit/Hyperactivity DisorderADHD

Core targets
  • Dopamine System DisruptionHypodopaminergic reward signaling drives novelty-seeking, boredom intolerance and difficulty sustaining effort.
  • Social HierarchyYears of criticism and comparison leave intense rejection sensitivity and a felt loss of standing.
  • Evaluation of PerformanceA long history of negative feedback on performance breeds shame and avoidance of tasks.
  • AccomplishmentEarned, visible wins rebuild self-efficacy that repeated unfinished work has eroded.
  • SleepDelayed sleep onset and short sleep are very common and worsen attention and mood.
  • Circadian RhythmA delayed circadian phase often underlies the late nights and hard mornings.
  • Physical ActivityExercise acutely improves attention and executive control.
Also consider
Substance UseContent of FoodRelationship SkillsCommitment
Executive Functioning is central here. In the TTIM it is a cross-cutting area rather than a target: scaffold it (external structure, reminders, environment design) instead of assuming it can be permanently trained.
Neurodevelopmental

Autism Spectrum DisorderASD

Core targets
  • Experiencing ConnectionFeeling understood and connected on the client's own terms — autism is a named reason this target exists.
  • Social Homeostatic NeedsLoneliness is common even when connection is wanted; fit and quality matter more than quantity.
  • Control / Demand / PredictabilityDistress with unexpected change, a strong need for sameness, and for some, intense demand avoidance.
  • Chronic SNS ActivationSensory overload, the cost of masking, and autistic burnout keep the nervous system running hot.
  • Affect ToleranceAlexithymia and interoceptive differences make emotions hard to read early, leading to meltdowns or shutdowns.
  • SleepInsomnia is very common and amplifies sensory sensitivity and emotion dysregulation.
Also consider
Social HierarchyRespondent ConditioningRelationship SkillsHow You Make Others FeelContent of FoodGut Microbiome (Probiotics / Prebiotics)Circadian RhythmMeaning for LivingLooking Forward to ExperiencesAccomplishmentAttention Regulation
Executive Functioning differences are common. In the TTIM it is a cross-cutting area rather than a target: scaffold it with structure, visual schedules and environment design rather than training it away.
Note: The goal is wellbeing — less sensory overload, burnout and loneliness — not appearing neurotypical. Relationship Skills and How You Make Others Feel are two-way work, targeted only where the client wants change.
Note: People with ASD often process social hierarchy with effort, through explicit, bottom-up reasoning, whereas people with personality disorders tend to show a more implicit hyper-attunement to it. In ASD the concern tends to be outcome-based; in personality disorders it tends to be ego-based. So a person with ASD may miss being “put down” while focused on getting the outcome, whereas a person with a personality disorder may miss the outcome entirely because they are focused on having been put down.
Schizophrenia Spectrum

Schizophrenia

Core targets
  • Beliefs / Education / Experience / LanguageHow delusions and voices are appraised shapes the distress they cause (the focus of CBT for psychosis).
  • Attention RegulationAberrant salience and attention captured by voices or threat cues.
  • Dopamine System DisruptionDysregulated dopamine signaling is central to positive symptoms and the target of antipsychotics.
  • Substance UseCannabis and stimulants raise relapse risk; smoking rates and their health toll are very high.
  • Social Homeostatic NeedsIsolation and shrinking networks often follow illness onset and negative symptoms.
  • Looking Forward to ExperiencesNegative symptoms blunt anticipatory pleasure and the drive to pursue goals.
  • Physical ActivityAntipsychotic-related metabolic syndrome drives a large life-expectancy gap; movement is protective.
  • Content of FoodDiet quality is a lever against antipsychotic-related weight gain and insulin resistance.
Also consider
SleepInflammationRelationship SkillsMeaning for Living
Schizophrenia Spectrum

Schizoaffective Disorder

Core targets
  • Beliefs / Education / Experience / LanguageAppraisal of psychotic experiences shapes distress and functioning.
  • Attention RegulationAberrant salience and attention captured by voices or threat cues.
  • Dopamine System DisruptionDopamine dysregulation underlies psychotic symptoms and is a primary medication target.
  • Substance UseSubstance use destabilizes both psychotic and mood symptoms.
  • Social Homeostatic NeedsIsolation commonly follows illness onset and negative symptoms.
  • SleepSleep loss is an early warning sign and trigger for mood and psychotic episodes.
  • Circadian RhythmStable daily rhythms help prevent manic and depressive episodes.
  • Physical ActivityMedication-related metabolic risk makes movement a core health target.
  • Content of FoodDiet quality counters medication-related weight gain and insulin resistance.
Also consider
Good Things Are ComingLooking Forward to ExperiencesCommitmentRefined Sugars / Fructose Consumption
Bipolar

Bipolar I & II DisordersBD

Core targets
  • Circadian RhythmDisrupted social and circadian rhythms precede episodes — the focus of IPSRT.
  • SleepSleep loss can trigger mania; protecting sleep is a frontline relapse-prevention strategy.
  • Dopamine System DisruptionReward hypersensitivity: goal pursuit and goal-attainment events can tip into hypomania.
  • Substance UseHighly comorbid and destabilizing to mood and medication response.
  • CommitmentSustained commitment to medication and routine during well periods prevents relapse.
Also consider
Physical ActivityContent of FoodRefined Sugars / Fructose ConsumptionEvaluation of Performance
Executive Functioning often dips during and between episodes — scaffold it rather than assume it will return on its own.
Depressive

Major Depressive DisorderMDD

Core targets
  • Looking Forward to ExperiencesAnhedonia: nothing ahead feels worth anticipating.
  • Good Things Are ComingHopelessness about the future maintains withdrawal and low effort.
  • AccomplishmentBehavioral activation works through earned wins that restart reward learning.
  • Cognitive DistortionsThe negative cognitive triad biases how the self, world and future are read.
  • SleepInsomnia and hypersomnia both maintain depression and predict relapse.
  • InflammationAn inflammatory subtype is linked to fatigue, anhedonia and treatment resistance.
Also consider
Physical ActivitySocial Homeostatic NeedsDopamine System DisruptionMeaning for LivingCircadian Rhythm
Depressive

Persistent Depressive DisorderPDD

Core targets
  • Beliefs / Education / Experience / LanguageLong-standing self-schemas — “this is just who I am” — keep low mood entrenched.
  • Agency Over FutureYears of low mood breed learned helplessness about changing outcomes.
  • Experiencing ConnectionChronic depression often involves interpersonal detachment (a focus of CBASP).
  • How You Make Others FeelLearning one's impact on others is central to CBASP's interpersonal work.
  • Meaning for LivingA reason for living sustains effort when mood offers little reward.
Also consider
Good Things Are ComingCommitmentPhysical ActivityInflammationSleep
Anxiety

Social Anxiety Disorder

Core targets
  • Social HierarchyFear of negative evaluation is fundamentally a fear of losing social standing.
  • Evaluation of PerformanceSocial situations are experienced as performances that are being judged.
  • Attention RegulationSelf-focused attention fuels anxiety and blocks disconfirming evidence (Clark & Wells).
  • Respondent ConditioningAvoidance and safety behaviors keep conditioned fear alive; exposure updates it.
  • Social Homeostatic NeedsAvoidance leaves social needs chronically unmet.
Also consider
Cognitive DistortionsRelationship SkillsSubstance Use
Anxiety

Agoraphobia

Core targets
  • Respondent ConditioningSituational and interoceptive exposure extinguish conditioned fear of places and sensations.
  • Cognitive DistortionsCatastrophic misinterpretation of bodily sensations drives panic and avoidance.
  • Affect ToleranceBuilding tolerance for physiological arousal without escaping.
  • Control / Demand / PredictabilityFear centers on being trapped or unable to get help.
  • Chronic SNS ActivationElevated baseline arousal keeps the alarm system primed.
Also consider
Social Homeostatic NeedsLooking Forward to ExperiencesPhysical ActivitySubstance Use
Anxiety

Generalized Anxiety DisorderGAD

Core targets
  • Control / Demand / PredictabilityIntolerance of uncertainty is a primary maintaining factor.
  • Chronic SNS ActivationChronic tension, restlessness and insomnia reflect sustained sympathetic activation.
  • Attention RegulationAttention is biased toward threat and captured by worry.
  • AcceptanceAccepting realistic probabilities rather than seeking certainty.
  • Affect ToleranceWorry can function to avoid sharp emotional shifts (the contrast-avoidance model).
Also consider
Cognitive DistortionsEvaluation of PerformanceSleepSubstance Use
Obsessive-Compulsive

Obsessive-Compulsive DisorderOCD

Core targets
  • Respondent ConditioningExposure and response prevention breaks the obsession–compulsion conditioning loop.
  • Control / Demand / PredictabilityIntolerance of uncertainty drives checking and reassurance seeking.
  • Locus of ControlInflated responsibility for preventing harm.
  • Beliefs / Education / Experience / LanguageThought–action fusion and overestimation of threat.
  • Affect ToleranceSitting with anxiety, disgust or “not just right” feelings without performing the compulsion.
Also consider
AcceptanceAttention Regulation
Trauma & Stressor

Posttraumatic Stress DisorderPTSD

Core targets
  • Sympathetic Learning History / TraumaThe trauma history itself sensitizes the nervous system to threat.
  • Respondent ConditioningFear conditioning and failed extinction keep reminders dangerous.
  • Chronic SNS ActivationHyperarousal, startle and hypervigilance.
  • Beliefs / Education / Experience / LanguageTrauma-related “stuck points” about safety, trust, blame and worth (the focus of CPT).
  • SleepNightmares and insomnia maintain symptoms and are independently treatable.
  • Affect ToleranceA narrowed window of tolerance for trauma-related emotion.
Also consider
Experiencing ConnectionSubstance UseAgency Over FuturePhysical ActivityInflammation
Trauma & Stressor

Adjustment Disorder

Core targets
  • Control / Demand / PredictabilityThe stressor has abruptly shifted demand and predictability.
  • AcceptanceComing to terms with a changed reality.
  • Agency Over FutureRestoring a sense of influence over what comes next.
  • Social Homeostatic NeedsMobilizing support during the transition.
  • SleepStress-related sleep disruption amplifies distress.
Also consider
Meaning for LivingPhysical ActivityCommitment
Note: Usually time-limited and strengths-based: often fewer targets, worked more briefly.

Personality · Cluster B

Antisocial Personality Disorder

Core targets
  • Dopamine System DisruptionSensation and reward seeking with low sensitivity to punishment.
  • Substance UseVery high comorbidity; intoxication amplifies impulsive harm.
  • How You Make Others FeelLimited awareness of one's impact on others.
  • Locus of ControlExternalizing blame for outcomes.
  • Purpose to OthersProsocial roles build an identity incompatible with harming others.
  • CommitmentSustained, values-driven willingness to change.

Personality · Cluster B

Borderline Personality DisorderBPD

Core targets
  • Affect ToleranceEmotional intensity and slow return to baseline — the heart of DBT.
  • Social HierarchyRejection sensitivity and unstable self-worth react strongly to perceived devaluation.
  • Sympathetic Learning History / TraumaInvalidating or traumatic environments shape the biosocial vulnerability.
  • Relationship SkillsInterpersonal effectiveness for stable, less chaotic relationships.
  • Experiencing ConnectionFear of abandonment and insecure attachment.
  • Social Homeostatic NeedsUnmet needs for closeness and fear of being alone intensify distress and crises.
  • Substance UseSubstance use as impulsive emotional escape.

Personality · Cluster B

Narcissistic Personality DisorderNPD

Core targets
  • Social HierarchyCentral: worth is contingent on status and rank.
  • Evaluation of PerformanceAchievement props up a fragile self-image.
  • How You Make Others FeelEmpathic accuracy and awareness of impact.
